Reliable systems are built before the incident.

Good infrastructure work is not measured by how often a support team comes to the rescue. It is measured by how rarely avoidable incidents happen in the first place.

That means understanding what the systems do, removing recurring causes of failure, planning capacity before resources run short, keeping software and configuration under control, and making recovery a tested procedure rather than an assumption. It also means leaving the people responsible for those systems with better knowledge and better tools than they had before.

The result is infrastructure that becomes quieter: fewer surprises, fewer repeated problems, clearer decisions, and more time for your team to focus on the work that matters to the business.

Bring us the problem, not a predefined service.

You may have a production issue nobody has been able to explain. A group of Linux servers may have grown without enough documentation or consistent management. Capacity may be approaching its limit. A key engineer may be leaving, or your development team may need systems expertise that does not make sense to hire full-time.

You may also be looking further ahead: improving resilience, preparing for growth, reviewing security, making recovery dependable, or giving your own team the knowledge and practices to operate more confidently.

Start with the situation as you see it. We will help define the technical work, the responsibility each side should hold, and a way of collaborating that is useful to both teams.

How we work

The right level of responsibility for your organisation.

There is no required journey and no standard package. An engagement can be small and specific, broad and long-term, or change shape as the organisation changes.

01 Solve a difficult problem

We investigate faults, instability, performance bottlenecks, failed changes, network behaviour, or infrastructure that has become too risky to touch. The goal is not a temporary workaround. We establish what is happening, correct the underlying cause where possible, and leave a clear record of the decisions and changes.

02 Improve an existing platform

We bring inherited or fast-growing systems under control: standardising configuration, improving monitoring, reviewing access, planning capacity, testing recovery, and automating work that should not depend on manual repetition.

03 Strengthen your team

We work as part of your existing technical operation, inside its tools and processes. We can provide senior judgement, share responsibility for particular systems, review plans, support difficult changes, or train colleagues so that more knowledge remains inside the organisation.

04 Take responsibility for the infrastructure

Where that is the right model, we can operate the Linux infrastructure on an ongoing basis: maintaining context, planning and carrying out changes, monitoring health and capacity, and improving the systems over time. The scope is agreed around what the business actually needs.

What sits behind reliable Linux systems

Systems, networks, security, and resilience.

Linux systems and virtualisation

Provisioning, operating system lifecycle, configuration, identity, storage, virtual machines, hypervisors, performance, and capacity. We work with the platform you already depend on and improve it where improvement has a clear purpose.

Networks and secure access

Firewalls, segmentation, routing, VPNs, reverse proxies, TLS, and the paths between users, services, sites, and providers. Designed to remain understandable as well as secure.

Operational resilience

Monitoring, patching, backups, restore testing, continuity planning, and recovery. The disciplines that make failures less likely and reduce their impact when they do happen.

Security and control

Hardening, access boundaries, audit remediation, vulnerability response, and configuration management. Security treated as part of daily infrastructure operation, not a product applied once and forgotten.

Automation and documentation

Repeatable configuration, reviewable changes, useful operating procedures, and knowledge that belongs to your organisation. Automation makes routine work consistent; documentation makes responsibility transferable.
